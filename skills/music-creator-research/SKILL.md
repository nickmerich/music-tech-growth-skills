# Music Creator Research

## Segments (start here, then prove or kill them)

| Segment | Job to be done | Typical worry |
|---------|----------------|---------------|
| Beatmaker / producer | Make something that sounds finished, fast | "Will it sound like everyone else?" |
| Artist who produces | Get an idea down before it's gone | Workflow friction, CPU |
| Mix / mastering engineer | Reliable results on client work | Quality, recall, CPU, support |
| Songwriter / topliner | Demo vocals or parts without a session | Realism, rights |
| Educator / YouTuber | Something worth teaching | Novelty, affiliate economics |
| Hobbyist | Fun and progress | Price, complexity |

Add: DAW, OS, genre, experience, budget, where they hang out.

## Mining

Search where creators talk honestly: subreddits for producers and specific DAWs, Gearspace, KVR, YouTube review comments, Discord servers, plugin store reviews. For each competitor and your own product, capture:

- Exact phrases (quote them, link them)
- Jobs ("I need choir pads for a gospel intro")
- Objections (price, CPU, iLok, formats, Windows/Mac support, AI concerns, rights)
- Switching triggers ("I finally bought it after hearing it on...")

Tag each quote: segment, theme, sentiment, source URL. Count themes; don't summarize from vibes.

## Interview script (20 minutes)

1. Walk me through the last session where you used [category].
2. What were you trying to make?
3. What did you try first? What annoyed you?
4. How did you find [product]? What almost stopped you from buying or trying?
5. What did you make in the first session? Did you keep it?
6. Who did you show it to?

## Output

Segment table with evidence counts, a quote bank with links, top five objections with the fix each needs, and the one segment to focus on next.

## AI products

Creators care a lot about how AI tools are trained, who owns output, and whether it replaces musicians. Capture those concerns directly and answer them truthfully in product pages; never overstate rights you don't have.
